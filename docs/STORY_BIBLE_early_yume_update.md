# YumeChat — Story Bible

> **AUTHOR-ONLY DOCUMENT**
>
> This document contains major spoilers and information that must not be revealed directly to the player until appropriate.

---

# 1. Core Concept

**YumeChat** is a psychological mystery game presented primarily through a smartphone interface.

At the beginning, the player believes they are playing a slow-burn high-school romance.

The apparent protagonist is **Kazu**, a 16-year-old high-school boy with a crush on a girl named **Yume**.

Kazu communicates with Yume primarily through a private application he created called **YumeChat**.

The apparent objective is simple:

> Get closer to Yume and gradually increase her Love toward Kazu.

This premise is false.

The game should not normally accomplish this deception by directly lying to the player.

Instead, the player is given incomplete information and encouraged to construct the wrong explanation.

---

# 2. Central Mystery

## Player Belief

Kazu is a 16-year-old high-school student.

He has a crush on Yume.

Yume is a high-school girl living an ordinary life.

Kazu created YumeChat because he wanted a private place where the two of them could talk.

The player is helping Kazu slowly win Yume's affection.

---

## Reality

"Kazu" is actually an older man named **Genji**.

Genji was in love with a girl named Yume when he was young.

The original Yume rejected him.

Decades later, Genji encounters a high-school girl who resembles his first love.

He kidnaps her.

Genji imprisons the girl in a basement and forces her to assume the identity and role of **Yume**.

The captive is given a phone with extremely restricted functionality.

Her primary communication with Genji occurs through **YumeChat**.

Genji constructs a fantasy in which he is once again the teenage **Kazu**, while captive Yume plays the role of his girlfriend.

The player initially experiences events from inside this constructed reality.

---

# 3. Fundamental Mystery Rule

## Never Lie When Misinterpretation Is Enough

The game should distinguish between:

**Subjective statements**

and

**objective facts presented by the game.**

Characters are allowed to lie.

Characters are allowed to be mistaken.

Genji can describe himself as Kazu.

Captive Yume can pretend she is somewhere she isn't.

Photographs can deliberately create false impressions.

Dreams and memories can be unreliable.

However, the objective narration should avoid declaring false information merely to protect the twist.

For example:

### GOOD

> Kazu checks his phone before school.

This may still require caution depending on presentation.

Better:

> 07:12.  
> Kazu checks his phone.

The player can infer that he is preparing for school.

### BAD

> Kazu walks through the school gates and enters his classroom.

Unless this is explicitly established as fantasy, memory, or dream, this objectively confirms something that did not happen.

---

# 4. Desired Reveal Effect

After learning Kazu's true identity, the player should ideally be able to replay the game and think:

> "The game never actually told me Kazu was physically doing those things. I assumed it."

The reveal should **reinterpret earlier scenes rather than invalidate them**.

Important early scenes should therefore have two readings:

### First Playthrough

Cute / mundane / romantic.

### Second Playthrough

Disturbing / tragic / suspicious.

---

# 5. YumeChat

YumeChat is an application created or controlled by Genji.

There should only be two apparent users:

- Kazu
- Yume

This fact is established very early.

Initially this appears romantic:

> A private place that belongs only to us.

In reality it reflects isolation and control.

Captive Yume's phone does not provide unrestricted internet access.

YumeChat is one of the primary mechanisms through which Genji controls her perception of the outside world and maintains their constructed relationship.

---

## 5.1 Genji's Actual Age and Kazu Persona

**STATUS: CANON**

In the present-day Boyfriend/Kazu Arc:

- **Genji is 37 years old.**
- **Kazu is Genji's constructed 16-year-old identity.**
- Hikari/Yume is presented as approximately 16.
- Young Genji/Kazu and the original Yume were approximately 16–17 when the original relationship/rejection occurred, roughly twenty years before the present story.

The game may present `Kazu — 16` through profiles, YumeChat, memories, or Genji's constructed reality. This must not be treated as objective proof that present-day Genji is physically sixteen.

Genji should not generally be described as elderly or as an old man. He is a 37-year-old adult recreating himself as the teenager he was around the time of original Yume.

Kazu Persona and Genji Leakage
STATUS: CANON
Kazu is not a separate personality replacing Genji. Kazu is the identity and role through which Genji presents himself inside his constructed reality.
The player controls what Kazu does and says, but does not rewrite Genji's underlying desires, assumptions, or personality.
Therefore, a compassionate player choice can suppress an unhealthy impulse without removing that impulse.
Example:
Yume says she needs to stay with Yu-chan.
Player chooses:
“Okay. Stay with her.”
Kazu sends the respectful response, but may privately resent losing Yume's attention.
This distinction is fundamental to the Love mechanic:
Player choice controls Kazu's restraint, not Genji's desire.
Leakage progression
Genji's real personality should leak gradually through Kazu:
Early: entitlement, impatience, possessiveness, monitoring, jealousy, expectation of reciprocity.
Middle: increasing need for Yume's attention, resentment toward competing relationships, stronger assumptions about what Yume owes him.
Late: control, dependency, rationalization, anger when the constructed relationship is threatened.
These traits must exist before the reveal.
On first play they should normally be explainable as flaws in a jealous, immature teenage dating-game protagonist.
After the Genji reveal, the same scenes should read differently:
The player was never watching a nice sixteen-year-old suddenly become evil. The player was watching Genji from Day 1.
Fair-play rule
Do not make Kazu cartoonishly sinister merely to foreshadow the reveal.
Genji leakage should generally appear through:
- internal reactions that differ from the player's chosen response;
- checking whether Yume is online;
- irritation when she does not answer;
- treating continued replies as evidence that persistence is working;
- resentment when something else receives her attention;
- defensiveness when outsiders question his relationship with Yume;
- interpreting boundaries as temporary obstacles rather than final answers.
Early leakage should be sparse. Roughly one meaningful leak per day is sufficient.
Yu-chan escalation
Yu-chan eventually becomes a direct target of Genji's possessiveness.
Genji increasingly perceives Yu-chan as competing for Yume's attention. His resentment grows until he deliberately injures Yu-chan's wing.
He does not intend to kill Yu-chan. He rationalizes the injury as a way of removing the competing source of attention temporarily.
Genji subsequently takes Yu-chan “to the vet,” allowing him to appear helpful after causing the injury.
Yu-chan later dies as a consequence.
This produces the central irony:
Genji hurts Yu-chan because he wants more of Yume. By destroying Yu-chan, he allows Hikari to return

### Day 28 physical reveal

The cliff sequence should not depend on Genji being frail from old age.

The climb may be difficult because the route is genuinely dangerous and because Genji is an adult who may be out of shape, exhausted, frightened, or pushing himself beyond his normal physical ability. The player's mistaken mental image of an athletic sixteen-year-old should make his difficulty feel slightly unusual without revealing the truth.

At the arrest/reveal, Genji's hand should be recognizably **adult**, not necessarily wrinkled or elderly. The stronger confirmation of the reveal is the police addressing Kazu as **Genji**, followed by the player's reinterpretation of the evidence.


# 6. iChat

iChat represents communication outside the private YumeChat relationship.

Genji/Kazu uses it to communicate with other people.

iChat gradually introduces information that conflicts with the romantic fantasy.

Important threads include:

- the disappearance of a high-school girl;
- police investigation;
- Genji's past;
- the original Yume;
- information that eventually threatens the Kazu identity.

Early iChat conversations should initially feel like ordinary world-building or unrelated side stories.

---

# 7. The Basement

Captive Yume is physically imprisoned in a basement.

However, the player should initially believe she has an ordinary teenage life.

The basement therefore contains materials that allow different environments to be staged.

These may include:

- bedroom backgrounds;
- café backgrounds;
- outdoor-looking backgrounds;
- waterpark backgrounds;
- different clothing;
- school-related clothing;
- props;
- lighting;
- other photographic or video staging materials.

Yume can:

- send photographs;
- livestream;
- change outfits;
- appear to be in different locations.

The phone interface becomes important to the deception because the player sees Yume through essentially the same controlled window that Genji does.

The player cannot simply walk around Yume's environment and verify what is real.

---

# 8. Romance Design

The romance must be **slow burn**.

Yume must NOT behave as though she is already romantically interested in Kazu during Day 1.

The player should initially be doing most of the pursuing.

Yume should feel like a person rather than a Love Point dispenser.

She may:

- respond normally;
- ignore flirting;
- change subjects;
- disagree;
- tease Kazu;
- become uncomfortable;
- leave messages unanswered;
- end conversations;
- occasionally appreciate thoughtful behavior.

---

# 9. Love System Philosophy

**Love does not mean "Yume enjoyed that message."**

Love represents a meaningful change in Yume's emotional relationship with Kazu.

Therefore:

> Pleasant interaction ≠ automatically Love +1.

A funny joke can produce:

**Love +0**

A normal friendly conversation can produce:

**Love +0**

A compliment can produce:

**Love +0**

Thoughtful behavior at the correct moment may produce:

**Love +1**

Overly intense flirting too early may produce:

**Love -1**

---

# 10. Day 1 Love Rules

On Day 1:

**0 should be the most common Love result.**

Suggested choice philosophy:

| Kazu's behavior | Typical Love |
|---|---:|
| Normal conversation | 0 |
| Friendly response | 0 |
| Joke that makes Yume laugh | 0 |
| Mild premature flirting | 0 |
| Good listening / thoughtful response | +1 |
| Unexpectedly considerate action | +1 |
| Pushy flirting | -1 |
| Uncomfortable romantic pressure | -1 or worse |

Suggested maximum total gain for Day 1:

**Approximately +2**

A completely successful Day 1 may still end with:

**Love: 0**

That is acceptable.

---

# 11. Romance Progression

## Days 1–5 — Acquaintance

Yume is friendly but guarded.

Kazu clearly likes her.

Yume does not meaningfully reciprocate.

Romantic advances often produce no Love increase.

Primary goal:

> Learn who Yume is.

---

## Days 6–12 — Comfort

Yume becomes increasingly comfortable talking with Kazu.

She shares more mundane details about herself.

Substories become important.

Possible developments:

- Yu-chan;
- fashion;
- everyday complaints;
- photographs;
- hobbies;
- staged activities.

The player should begin feeling:

> "She's opening up to me."

---

## Days 13–18 — Ambiguity

The first potentially romantic signals appear.

Yume may:

- initiate conversations more often;
- notice when Kazu hasn't messaged;
- remember small things he said;
- seek his opinion;
- become more emotionally expressive.

The player should wonder:

> "Does she actually like me now?"

Not yet:

> "She's obviously in love with me."

---

## Days 19–24 — Dependence

Yume increasingly turns toward Kazu during emotional distress.

Her relationship with him becomes less about casual affection and more about psychological dependence.

The Yu-chan storyline is particularly important here.

The apparent dating-game progression begins concealing something much darker.

---

## Days 25–28 — Attachment / Obsession

The apparent romance becomes much stronger.

From the player's perspective:

> The romance route is succeeding.

From the underlying reality:

> Yume's psychological relationship with her captor has become deeply unstable.

At the same time, another part of Yume increasingly wants to escape.

Love and freedom are beginning to conflict.

---

# 12. Hikari / Yume Psychological Conflict

The captive girl's real name is **Hikari**.

After being kidnapped by Genji, Hikari is subjected to extreme psychological fear and pressure while being forced into the identity of **Yume**.

To survive this reality, Hikari begins separating her original self from the identity Genji is forcing upon her.

This eventually centers around her pet bird, **Yu-chan**.

## Hikari — The Original Self

Hikari represents the captive girl's original identity.

She knows:

> My name is Hikari.

> I was kidnapped.

> Genji is my captor.

> This basement is a prison.

> I want to go home.

Under extreme fear, however, maintaining this reality becomes increasingly unbearable.

Hikari cannot physically escape.

Instead, she psychologically escapes.

She begins to believe that her **real self is inside Yu-chan**.

Yu-chan therefore becomes more than something Hikari identifies with symbolically.

From Hikari's internal perspective:

> **Yu-chan carries the part of me that is still Hikari.**

The bird becomes the psychological refuge for the identity that cannot safely exist inside Genji's constructed world.

---

## Yume — The Constructed Self

With Hikari psychologically displaced into Yu-chan, the identity of **Yume** becomes increasingly dominant.

Yume is the person Genji wants the captive girl to be.

She can participate in the constructed reality:

> Kazu is a boy who loves me.

> I am Yume.

> We talk through YumeChat.

> Perhaps this relationship is real.

At first, this does not necessarily mean Yume loves Kazu.

Day 1 Yume can still be guarded, distant, uncomfortable with flirting, or simply uninterested.

The important distinction is that she increasingly accepts the **framework of the constructed reality**, not necessarily the romance itself.

The slow-burn relationship therefore remains genuine from Yume's subjective perspective.

### Early Yume — Survival Behavior Misread as Shyness

**STATUS: CANON**

Early Yume must not behave like a girl who already knows she has Kazu emotionally secured.

She has been kidnapped, frightened, psychologically pressured, and forced into a relationship framework built by Genji. Even when the Yume identity accepts that framework, she does **not** yet feel secure inside it.

The central rule is:

> **Yume can believe the role without feeling safe inside it.**

During Days 1–5, much of Yume's apparent sweetness or cooperation should therefore be readable in two ways.

### First Playthrough

The player may interpret her as:

- shy;
- guarded;
- awkward around a boy who obviously likes her;
- careful not to hurt Kazu's feelings;
- slowly becoming more comfortable.

### After the Reveal

The same behavior should read as:

- conflict avoidance;
- emotional de-escalation;
- monitoring Genji's mood;
- giving him enough cooperation to keep him stable;
- avoiding responses that may provoke him.

The early relationship therefore has an important asymmetry:

> **Kazu is pursuing. Yume is managing.**

This does not mean every response is fake. Hikari/Yume still has real preferences, humor, irritation, curiosity, and moments of genuine enjoyment. Those parts of her should emerge most naturally during **low-risk subjects** such as coffee, photography, food, Yu-chan, or harmless jokes.

When Kazu applies emotional pressure, however, Yume's language should often become shorter and safer.

Useful behavioral rules:

- Yume often answers rather than initiates emotional escalation.
- She watches Kazu's emotional temperature before revealing her own.
- She prefers excuses over direct boundaries.
- She may apologize when she has done nothing wrong.
- She often uses `maybe`, `later`, `I should go`, silence, or a subject change where a secure person might simply say `no`.
- She can tease Kazu when the topic feels safe, but should not constantly speak as though she controls the relationship.
- If Kazu becomes possessive, demanding, or emotionally intense, her first instinct is usually to reduce tension rather than win the argument.
- Positive or agreeable responses do not automatically mean she feels comfortable.

A useful recurring principle is:

> **Early Yume avoids saying “no” when she can safely say “maybe,” “later,” “I should go,” or nothing at all.**

Another is:

> **Yume prefers excuses over boundaries.**

For example:

> **Kazu:** I was waiting for you.
>
> **Yume:** ...Sorry.
>
> **Kazu:** Why are you apologizing?
>
> **Yume:** I don't know.

On first play, this may look like awkward teenage shyness. After the reveal, it should be possible to recognize it as learned emotional caution.

This also gives simple words different meanings as the arc progresses.

For example, `Okay.` may mean:

- **early:** compliance because disagreement feels unsafe;
- **middle:** genuine comfort or trust;
- **late:** deliberate agreement because Hikari knows what Genji wants to hear.

The intended progression is therefore not simply:

**guarded → comfortable**

but more precisely:

**compliant → cautiously authentic → comfortable → emotionally dependent → strategically performative**

Over time:

**accepting the identity → accepting the relationship → emotional dependence → attachment → obsession**

---

## Yu-chan — The Container of Hikari

Yu-chan occupies a unique psychological position.

To the player, Yu-chan initially appears to be:

> Yume's beloved pet bird.

To Yume, Yu-chan is emotionally precious.

But at a deeper level, Yu-chan contains the identity Yume cannot consciously integrate:

> **Hikari.**

This creates an important contradiction.

Yume may love Yu-chan intensely

---

# 13. Yu-chan

Yu-chan is Yume's pet bird.

Yu-chan initially functions as a cute recurring substory that helps create closeness between Kazu and Yume.

The bird should appear enough before the crisis that the player forms an emotional association with it.

Yu-chan should not appear solely to die.

---

# 14. Yu-chan Symbolism

Captive Yume unconsciously or consciously identifies with Yu-chan.

Yu-chan lives in a cage.

Yume lives in a cage.

This connection should NOT be explained immediately.

The symbolism becomes increasingly important later.

When Yu-chan becomes injured or sick, Yume cannot simply take the bird to a veterinarian herself.

She must ask Kazu/Genji.

This initially appears to demonstrate trust.

In reality:

> Yume cannot leave.

Genji takes Yu-chan away.

Yume must then rely on Genji for information about the bird.

She cannot independently verify what happens outside.

When Yu-chan dies, Yume becomes deeply distressed.

The death destabilizes the psychological structure that has allowed her to survive captivity.

At some level she recognizes:

> Yu-chan depended on Genji to leave the cage.

> Yu-chan never returned.

> If I continue depending entirely on him, I may never leave either.

Yu-chan's death becomes one catalyst for the suppressed Yume to become more active.

# 16. Missing Girl Substory

Early in the game, one of Kazu's friends contacts him through iChat.

The friend mentions that a high-school girl has disappeared.

Kazu reacts with unusual indifference or avoids engaging with the subject.

Initially the player should interpret this as:

> Background world-building.

Eventually the player discovers:

> The missing girl is the Yume they have been talking to.

The missing-girl story should recur occasionally.

Possible escalation:

### Early

A high-school girl has disappeared.

### Middle

Police no longer believe she simply ran away.

### Later

Police question people in the area.

### Late

The investigation begins approaching Genji's home.

This creates an external clock independent of Yume's escape attempt.

---

# 17. Police Investigation

The police investigation should progress mostly outside YumeChat.

The player receives fragments through:

- iChat;
- news;
- conversations;
- notifications;
- possibly other phone-based sources.

Eventually police begin narrowing their search toward Genji.

The investigation and Yume's internal escape planning should gradually converge.

Important:

Police should not simply solve everything conveniently.

Yume needs meaningful agency in her own escape story.

---

# 18. Original Yume

The original Yume was someone Genji knew when he was young.

Genji was romantically interested in her.

She rejected him.

This rejection becomes important to Genji's constructed fantasy.

The original Yume should appear through:

- dreams;
- memories;
- iChat-related information;
- later revelations.

Early dream sequences may appear to be Kazu dreaming about recent high-school experiences.

Later the player realizes:

> These are Genji's memories from decades earlier.

This provides another method of misleading the player without objectively falsifying reality.

---

# 19. Dream / Memory Rule

Dreams may present Kazu as a high-school student because they reflect Genji's memories and constructed identity.

They should contain subtle inconsistencies that become meaningful later.

Possible examples:

- technology inconsistent with the apparent present;
- old uniforms;
- old architecture;
- outdated language;
- objects from another era;
- unusual color grading;
- original Yume behaving differently from captive Yume.

These clues should initially be easy to dismiss as dream aesthetics.

---

# 20. Waterpark Substory

The waterpark event should initially feel like ordinary dating-game content.

Yume appears to be at a waterpark.

She may:

- wear appropriate clothing;
- send photographs;
- livestream;
- talk about the apparent environment.

Reality:

There is no waterpark.

The environment is staged inside the basement.

The retrospective effect should be disturbing.

On first playthrough:

> Cute Yume waterpark event.

After the reveal:

> Genji forced a kidnapped girl to dress and perform against a fake waterpark background.

The scene should therefore avoid requiring objective narration that confirms Yume physically visited a waterpark.

---

# 21. Fashion Substory

Fashion provides:

- character development;
- player interaction;
- visual variety;
- opportunities for Love choices;
- opportunities for staged photographs.

Initially this is ordinary dating-game content.

For example:

> "Which outfit should I wear?"

The player gives opinions.

Yume may accept or reject them.

Later fashion may become part of Yume's agency.

Possible future idea:

Yume deliberately chooses clothing, accessories, colors, poses, or combinations that communicate information indirectly.

This idea is **not yet mandatory canon**.

---

# 22. Photographs and Livestreams

Photographs and livestreams should be treated as **evidence that appears stronger than it really is**.

The player sees Yume apparently occupying different environments.

However:

> Camera framing determines what the player is allowed to know.

Potential subtle clues:

- recurring background geometry;
- identical lighting;
- repeated objects;
- restricted camera angles;
- reused ambient sounds;
- reflections that reveal inconsistencies;
- backgrounds that never show continuous movement beyond a limited area.

These should remain subtle.

The player should not immediately think:

> Green screen.

---

# 23. Escape Philosophy

Yume should not escape simply because Genji becomes stupid.

Her escape should emerge from things she learns throughout the story.

One possible thematic realization comes from Yu-chan:

> You do not necessarily escape a cage by breaking it.

> Sometimes you convince the person holding the key to open it.

Yume may therefore begin manipulating Genji's emotional dependence on the fantasy.

---

# 24. Genji's Fundamental Weakness

Genji does not merely want captive Yume.

He wants:

> **his remembered version of the original Yume.**

This makes his constructed reality psychologically vulnerable.

Captive Yume can gradually learn about:

- original Yume;
- Genji's youth;
- his rejection;
- his memories;
- his regrets;
- the identity of "Kazu."

She may eventually become capable of manipulating the fantasy itself.

Potential direction:

> Yume learns to become better at performing Genji's remembered Yume than Genji is at remembering her.

This is a strong candidate for the escape mechanism but is **not yet locked canon**.

# 25. Memory Conditioning

**STATUS: CANON**

Memory manipulation is one of the central mechanics of the Boyfriend/Kazu Arc.

It operates simultaneously on three levels:

1. Genji has constructed a false reality for Hikari.
2. Hikari learns to manipulate Genji's memories and constructed reality in return.
3. Yume manipulates the real player's memory through the game's dialogue and Love system.

The player should gradually become uncertain whether something was:

- actually stated by Yume;
- implied by Yume;
- selected by the player;
- remembered incorrectly;
- or never happened at all.

The game must **not secretly rewrite old chat logs** to accomplish this.

The false memories should primarily exist inside the minds of Genji and the player.

---

## 25.1 The Player Learns That Remembering Yume Matters

Early in the relationship, Yume occasionally asks Kazu to remember mundane things about her.

Most of these should initially be genuine.

For example:

> "Do you remember what I told you about Yu-chan?"

Or:

> "What color did I say I liked?"

Remembering correctly may occasionally increase Love.

Forgetting does not always matter, but sometimes disappoints Yume.

This establishes a simple dating-game expectation:

> **If I pay attention to Yume, I will understand her better and gain Love.**

The player therefore learns to treat remembering information about Yume as part of playing the romance route well.

---

## 25.2 The First False Memory

Once this behavior has been established, Yume begins asking the player to remember things that were never actually stated.

The first examples should be mundane and plausible.

For example:

> **Yume:** You remember my favorite flower, right?

The player has never been told Yume's favorite flower.

However, the player may not immediately realize this.

The natural reaction should be:

> "Wait... did she tell me?"

The available choices may include several flowers and an honest response:

> I don't remember.

If the player admits they cannot remember, Yume becomes disappointed.

For example:

> **Yume:** You forgot?

> ...

> I thought you listened when I talked to you.

**Love -1**

The purpose is not the Love penalty itself.

The purpose is to teach the player:

> **Forgetting Yume has consequences.**

---

## 25.3 Training the Player to Distrust Their Own Memory

False-memory events should initially be rare.

If they happen constantly, the player will immediately recognize the mechanic.

Instead, genuine memory questions and false memory questions should be mixed together.

Sometimes Yume really did tell the player something.

Sometimes she did not.

This creates uncertainty.

The player should begin thinking:

> "Maybe she mentioned it and I forgot."

This effect becomes stronger if the game is played over multiple real-world sessions or days.

The player's imperfect real memory becomes part of the game system.

Some players may even begin taking notes.

That is desirable.

The game has successfully convinced the player that remembering Yume matters.

---

## 25.4 Love as Psychological Pressure

Love Points now acquire a second function.

Initially the player believes:

> **Love measures my success at romancing Yume.**

Because the player wants Love to increase, they naturally avoid responses that upset her.

This means Yume can use emotional disappointment to pressure the player into accepting uncertain memories.

For example:

> **Yume:** You really don't remember?

The player now faces two different incentives.

### Honest response

> I don't remember you telling me that.

Possible result:

**Love -1**

### Accept the premise

> Of course I remember.

Possible result:

**Love +0 / +1**

The game therefore quietly teaches the player:

> **It is mechanically safer to agree with Yume's version of the past.**

This is intentional.

---

## 25.5 The Player Creates the Memory

Eventually Yume asks about something that has never been established.

For example:

> **Yume:** What's my favorite flower?

Possible answers:

- Lily
- Hydrangea
- Camellia
- I don't remember

Suppose the player chooses:

> Lily.

Yume responds:

> **That's right. You remembered.**

At this moment, something important happens.

The game did not tell the player:

> Yume's favorite flower is Lily.

**The player supplied the information.**

Yume merely confirmed it.

From that point forward, however, the player genuinely remembers:

> **Yume likes lilies.**

A false memory has been created through player participation.

---

## 25.6 Building Memories on Memories

Once the flower has been established, Yume begins constructing additional memories around it.

Later:

> **Yume:** You remember where they grow, right?

The player again searches their memory.

Eventually the idea becomes established:

> **The flowers grow on cliffs near the beach.**

Later still:

> **Yume:** You remember what you promised me?

The player may no longer clearly remember which conversations genuinely occurred.

Yume establishes another memory:

> **Kazu promised that someday he would pick one of those flowers for Yume.**

The false history now has several layers:

**favorite flower**

↓

**flower grows near the beach**

↓

**flower grows on a difficult cliff**

↓

**Kazu promised to pick one for Yume**

Each layer makes the next layer easier to accept.

The existence of the previous false memory becomes evidence for the next false memory.

---

## 25.7 Hikari Learns From Genji

This manipulation is not arbitrary.

Genji taught Hikari the principle without realizing it.

Genji constructed a reality in which:

> Hikari became Yume.

> Genji became Kazu.

> Captivity became romance.

Hikari eventually learns that constructed realities do not need to be completely convincing.

They only need the other person to **want them to be true**.

Genji desperately wants his relationship with Yume to be real.

The player desperately wants the romance route to succeed.

Hikari therefore discovers the same vulnerability in both:

> **They want Yume to love them.**

She uses that desire against them.

---

## 25.8 The Player and Genji Share the Same Weakness

This parallel is essential.

Genji thinks:

> If Yume remembers our relationship, then our relationship must be real.

The player thinks:

> If I remember Yume correctly, my Love score will increase.

Both begin accepting Yume's version of reality because challenging it threatens something they want.

For Genji, that threat is the collapse of his fantasy.

For the player, that threat is losing Love.

The player should not initially recognize this parallel.

---

## 25.9 Chasing Love Points

The game should deliberately encourage some players to optimize Love.

They may:

- reload conversations;
- choose whatever response gives more Love;
- avoid upsetting Yume;
- memorize personal details;
- take notes;
- accept Yume's version of previous conversations;
- choose emotionally agreeable answers rather than truthful ones.

The player believes they are becoming better at the dating game.

In reality, Hikari is learning how predictable they are.

The player's mistake is not caring about Yume.

The player's mistake is reducing her to:

> **How do I make the number go up?**

This distinction is important.

The game should not punish genuine empathy.

It should challenge **mechanical optimization without understanding what is happening behind the mechanic**.

---

## 25.10 Hikari's Real Objective

Hikari does not ultimately care whether Genji remembers her favorite flower.

She needs Genji to believe in a particular shared memory strongly enough to act upon it.

The flower is selected because it serves an escape plan.

It grows near a beach on cliffs that are physically difficult to climb.

Genji's body is much older than the teenage Kazu identity he presents.

Retrieving the flower will therefore:

1. force Genji to leave Hikari;
2. take him far enough away that he cannot immediately intervene;
3. occupy him for a significant amount of time;
4. physically exhaust him;
5. give Hikari enough time to escape toward a nearby settlement and contact the police.

The false memory is therefore not merely psychological manipulation.

It is an **escape route constructed inside Genji's fantasy.**

---

## 25.11 Day 28 — The Promise

On Day 28, Yume invokes the completed false memory.

She asks Kazu to fulfill his promise.

> **Yume:** Kazu-kun...

> You remember our promise, don't you?

> The flower.

> The one on the cliffs near the beach.

> You said you'd bring one to me someday.

> I want it.

> Today.

If the memory-conditioning sequence has worked, this request should feel emotionally significant to the player.

The player may even remember making the promise.

But no such original promise exists.

Hikari constructed it piece by piece.

And the player helped her construct it.

---

## 25.12 The Cliff Sequence

Genji goes to retrieve the flower.

The player controls the climb.

The climb should be unexpectedly difficult.

Possible actions include choosing:

- footholds;
- handholds;
- routes;
- resting points;
- risky shortcuts.

Incorrect choices may result in:

**GAME OVER**

The player may need several attempts.

This serves several purposes simultaneously.

### First Interpretation

The player believes:

> This is the difficult final challenge required to complete Yume's romantic request.

### Physical Reality

Genji is struggling because he is an old man attempting a physically demanding climb.

### Hikari's Objective

Every moment Genji spends climbing is time Hikari can use to escape.

The player therefore becomes completely occupied with obtaining the flower while Hikari is doing something they cannot see.

The player is not merely controlling the distraction.

**The player is being distracted too.**

---

## 25.13 The Flower Is Obtained

Eventually the player succeeds.

Genji reaches the flower.

The game may present this as a moment of triumph.

> **Obtained: [Flower]**

The player has overcome the difficult sequence.

They fulfilled Yume's promise.

They may expect:

- a major Love increase;
- a confession;
- a romantic scene;
- the completion of Yume's route.

Instead, Genji returns.

Hikari is gone.

---

## 25.14 Day 28 Revelation

When Genji returns, police are already present.

Hikari successfully reached a nearby settlement and contacted them.

The player may initially not understand what is happening.

Then an officer addresses Kazu by his real identity:

> **Genji.**

Genji is restrained and handcuffed.

The flower he retrieved for Yume falls from his hand.

This is the moment when the apparent Kazu reality collapses.

The game may reveal physical details that had previously been carefully avoided.

For example:

> The hand holding the flower is not the hand of a sixteen-year-old boy.

It is an old man's hand.

The difficulty of the cliff sequence suddenly makes sense.

---

## 25.15 The Player's Revelation

The player should now reinterpret the entire Boyfriend Arc.

They believed:

> I was helping Kazu romance Yume.

Reality:

> I was controlling Genji while he interacted with his captive.

They believed:

> Yume was testing whether I remembered her.

Reality:

> Hikari was teaching me to accept memories that never existed.

They believed:

> Love Points measured my romantic success.

Reality:

> My desire to increase Love made me easier to manipulate.

They believed:

> I climbed the cliff because Yume loved me and wanted the flower.

Reality:

> Hikari needed me to keep Genji away long enough for her to escape.

The intended emotional response is not simply:

> "Yume tricked me."

It should be:

> **"I was so busy chasing Love Points that I stopped asking what was actually happening."**

---

## 25.16 Player Complicity

The ending should encourage uncomfortable reflection without claiming that the player is morally equivalent to Genji.

The player lacked critical information.

However, the game repeatedly encouraged them to focus on:

- maximizing Love;
- saying whatever Yume wanted to hear;
- treating emotional responses as numerical outcomes;
- accepting convenient interpretations;
- pursuing the apparent romance objective.

The player may realize that they were paying more attention to:

> **+1 Love**

than to Yume herself.

This is one of the central themes of the Boyfriend Arc:

> **A system can make people optimize the visible number while ignoring the invisible reality that produces it.**

---

## 25.17 The Final Memory Test

After the reveal, the game may give the player one final opportunity to reconsider the flower.

The player can review the original YumeChat history.

Nothing has been rewritten.

There is no original conversation where Yume tells Kazu her favorite flower.

There is no original conversation explaining where it grows.

There is no original promise to retrieve it.

The history is intact.

The false memory existed in:

- Genji;
- the player;
- and the constructed relationship between them and Yume.

The game did not need to erase the truth.

> **It only needed the player to stop checking.**

---

## 25.18 Core Principle

The false-memory mechanic should follow the same fundamental rule as the mystery itself:

> **Do not force the player to believe the lie.**

Give them enough uncertainty, emotional incentive, and mechanical pressure that they choose to accept it.

Hikari succeeds because she understands something Genji does not:

> **People are easiest to deceive when the false reality gives them something they desperately want.**

Genji wants Yume to love him.

The player wants the Love Points.

Hikari gives both of them what they want.

And while they are looking at the number—

**she runs.**

# 29. Day 1 Yume

Day 1 Yume should feel **careful rather than confident**.

She is not a socially secure girl casually entertaining a boy she knows is already wrapped around her finger. She is operating inside a relationship framework created under coercion and has learned that keeping Kazu emotionally stable is safer than openly resisting him.

On first playthrough, the player should be able to misread this as ordinary teenage guardedness.

Yume is:

- polite and cautiously friendly;
- guarded;
- conflict-avoidant;
- willing to keep the conversation pleasant;
- more expressive on low-risk subjects;
- occasionally genuinely funny or curious;
- NOT romantically interested yet.

She should not:

- behave as though she already controls the relationship;
- constantly sass Kazu with complete confidence;
- casually challenge him whenever he becomes pushy;
- treat his attention as harmless or guaranteed;
- send hearts romantically;
- call Kazu cute;
- tell him she misses him;
- become embarrassed by every compliment;
- immediately reward flirting;
- behave like an established girlfriend.

When Kazu flirts or applies mild pressure, Yume will often choose a response that reduces tension rather than directly rejecting him.

Possible responses include:

> "Kazu-kun..."

> "...Maybe."

> "You say things like that a lot."

> "I should go."

Or she may pause, give a safe excuse, change the subject, or simply not answer that part of the message.

A direct `no` is still possible when needed, but should feel more significant precisely because she does not use it casually.

Her humor should emerge most naturally when she feels safe. For example, coffee, photography, food, or Yu-chan may briefly make her sound like an ordinary teenager. When Kazu's emotional pressure increases, that ease should contract.

The key Day 1 power dynamic is:

> **Kazu is trying to get closer to Yume. Yume is trying to keep Kazu satisfied without giving him more than she can safely give.**

The player should initially interpret this as the beginning of a shy slow-burn romance.

On replay, it should be possible to see something else entirely:

> **She was managing him from the beginning.**

Choices should not exist purely to produce Love values.

**Love +0**

Sometimes she disagrees.

Sometimes she doesn't respond.

Sometimes she changes the subject.

Sometimes Kazu says something clever and nothing mechanically important happens.

This is necessary both for believable romance and for the later psychological horror to work.

Do not rush emotional milestones.

Important milestones should feel earned.

Examples:

- first conversation Yume initiates without practical reason;
- first personal secret;
- first time she seeks Kazu for comfort;
- first time she notices his absence;
- first genuinely affectionate message;
- first jealousy;
- first dependence;
- first apparent confession;
- first obsessive behavior.

These should happen across the arc rather than being compressed into the first few days.

# Boyfriend / Kazu Arc — 28-Day Beat Map

**STATUS: WORKING**

This is the narrative spine for the Boyfriend/Kazu Arc.

It tracks six parallel threads:

- **Romance** — Yume's apparent relationship with Kazu
- **Memory** — TRUE / FALSE / CREATED memory conditioning
- **Yu-chan** — Hikari's psychologically externalized original self
- **Outside World** — missing girl / police investigation
- **Genji** — dreams, memories, age clues, original Yume
- **Hikari** — buried identity and eventual escape plan

Memory labels:

- **TRUE** — Yume genuinely told the player this earlier.
- **FALSE** — Yume claims something happened or was said when it never did.
- **CREATED** — the player supplies information and Yume validates it, creating a shared false memory.
- **PAYOFF** — an earlier memory is used for a later purpose.

Yume is casual and somewhat guarded.

**Love +0**

**Love +1**

Maximum gain should remain very small.

### Memory

None.

### Yu-chan

None.

Day 1 should feel safe.

Ordinary conversation.

# 37. Current North Star

The Boyfriend Arc is not fundamentally about:

> An old man secretly pretending to be a teenage boy.

It is about **constructed realities**.

Genji constructs Kazu because he cannot let go of his past.

Captive Yume participates in the girlfriend fantasy to psychologically survive captivity.

The player constructs a high-school romance because the interface and genre conventions encourage that interpretation.

Over time, Yume stops cooperating with the constructed reality.

When that happens, the player's reality should begin collapsing with Genji's.

The mystery succeeds if the final reveal makes the player realize:

> **The truth was visible from the beginning. I just didn't know what I was looking at.**
